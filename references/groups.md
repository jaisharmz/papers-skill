# Groups

Every entry on the path carries a group chip. It expands into what that lab works on generally,
their direction, and their thesis, with links out. It is on the entry rather than in a section,
because a section about labs below a reading list is a section nobody scrolls to.

Repos carry it too, and for a repo the maintaining group is often not the paper's group, which is
itself worth seeing.

## What a group node holds

- **the thesis, in their own words.** What they think the problem is and what they are betting on.
  Quoted or closely paraphrased, with the link.
- **what they are arguing against.** Usually stated more plainly than the thesis is.
- **direction.** What the last three papers have in common and where the next one is going.
- **the people.** The PI and the two or three whose names are on most of it.
- **their own list.** What the group considers its work, which differs from what cites them.
- **what they ship.** Whether their code is a research dump or a maintained system, per `repos.md`.
  A group whose repos are all dumps and a group that maintains what everyone uses are different
  places to work, and this is the cheapest place to see that.
- **where to read more.** Group page, the PI's own site, a recent talk. One live link each.

## Where to read a thesis off

**The discussion and limitations sections of their recent papers.** That is where a group says what
it thinks is still unsolved, in its own voice, under less pressure to sell than anywhere else.

A thesis written from their landing page is marketing. A thesis written from how other people
characterize them is worse, because it launders someone else's summary into the group's own view.

Position papers and long interviews are the next best source, since those are where senior people
state what they believe rather than what they proved.

## Disagreement

**Where two groups hold opposing theses, say so on both pages and name the paper pair.** That pair
feeds the dispute placement in `selection.md`, and knowing that positions three and four come from
two groups who have been arguing since 2023 changes how both get read.

Disagreement between credible groups is a finding, and it usually points straight at where the live
question is.

## The failure to avoid

**A confident thesis sentence for a group that does not have one.** Some groups publish across four
unrelated things because that is what the students wanted to work on, and manufacturing a through
line for them is inventing a fact about real people.

`thesis: none` with a reason is a real answer, the same way it is in `role-sourcing`. A run where a
third of the groups come back without one is working correctly.

## Sourcing

`industry-research/references/sourcing.md` governs. The rules that bite hardest here:

**Only name someone if you can point to a specific thing they wrote or built, and link it.** A list
of plausible researcher names with nothing attached looks like signal and is not.

**Do not guess at a current affiliation.** People move. If it came from a 2023 paper, say so.
Prefer the person's own page over an institutional announcement, since announcements are written
once and a personal site is where someone records that they left.

**Public professional information only.**

## What falls out for free

**Who to read next after this path.** Not who to email, which is `role-outreach`. Who is publishing
here now such that their next paper is worth watching for.
